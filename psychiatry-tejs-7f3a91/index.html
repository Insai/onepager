The assumed study — what the costs reason from

A psychiatric clinic monitors 10 patients at home with the Nexus EEG headband before medication start and again on stable medication. The question is feasibility: can multi-night home EEG capture sleep markers relevant to treatment response in this population?

The scientific rationale rests on three grounded observations. First, nearly all antidepressants suppress REM sleep — a robust, objectively measurable class effect (Riemann et al. 2019; Mayers & Baldwin 2005). Second, whether baseline sleep disturbance predicts treatment response is genuinely unresolved: findings are mixed (e.g. CO-MED, n=665, found no predictive value of self-reported insomnia), and nearly all existing evidence rests on self-report rather than objective EEG. Third, outcome is conventionally assessed 4–8 weeks after treatment start, and change within the first 2 weeks predicts stable response with high sensitivity (Szegedi et al. 2009, n=6,562).

Multi-night home EEG addresses exactly the evidence gap: objective sleep measurement, at home, before and during treatment. The specific drug class is a protocol decision.

Draft design: 2 × 5 sequential cohorts. Each patient spans ~7 weeks — a baseline recording week, then treatment as standard care, then a post recording week placed inside the conventional 4–8 week outcome-assessment window. Exact timing is a protocol decision. Five kits serve cohort 1, are reshipped, and serve cohort 2. Each patient receives a new headband; recorders and phones are cleaned and rotated.
What the timeline drives. The ~4–5 study months set the SIM subscriptions and the monitoring window; the 2 × 5 structure sets the kit count. It assumes each cohort's patients start within the same ~2 weeks — rolling recruitment stretches calendar time, which is cheap, but should be flagged in the protocol.

What would change the budget — and what wouldn't

Each card shows the assumed choice and the cost of choosing differently. Settle these in the protocol and the budget is final.

D1Sequential or parallel cohorts?
Five kits rotating through 2 × 5 keeps hardware minimal. Running all 10 patients at once needs 10 kits and halves the calendar.
Assumed · sequential 2 × 5 Parallel · +20,500 DKK hardware, −2 months
D2Measurement during treatment?
The draft records baseline and post only. There is a real scientific case for adding sampling during titration: clinical improvement within the first 2 weeks predicts stable response with ≥81% sensitivity (Szegedi et al. 2009), and REM suppression is the objective drug signature — whether early sleep-EEG change tracks response is exactly the kind of question a feasibility study can open.
Assumed · baseline + post Weekly sampling wks 1–5 · +~15,000 DKK support Continuous wear · +~20,000 DKK support + spares
D3When is the post window?
Week 4, 6 or 8 after treatment start — anywhere inside the conventional 4–8 week outcome-assessment window (Szegedi et al. 2009). Moves calendar time and SIM months slightly; the budget barely notices.
Assumed · ~week 5–6 Cost impact · negligible
D4Analysis depth — standard or bespoke?
Standard is the automated multi-night report with feasibility metrics. Bespoke adds endpoint-level scientific analysis, statistics and manuscript contribution.
Assumed · bespoke incl. publication Standard only · −37,500 DKK → total 173,250
D5Which drug class and clinical anchor?
SSRIs were mentioned but are unconfirmed. The clinical response measure — for example HAM-D or QIDS change — and who scores it defines the endpoint list, which sets the scientific analysis hours.

Sources & assumptions — what each claim rests on

Every number in this document is either confirmed, an estimate, or a stated assumption. Every scientific claim is pinned to a source.

01
REM suppression by antidepressants is a robust class effect.
Riemann D et al. Sleep, insomnia, and depression. Neuropsychopharmacology 2019;45:74–89 — doi:10.1038/s41386-019-0411-y. Mayers AG & Baldwin DS. Antidepressants and their effect on sleep. Hum Psychopharmacol 2005;20:533–59 (systematic review) — doi:10.1002/hup.726.
02
Baseline sleep as a response predictor — mixed evidence, and the gap this study addresses.
Sung SC et al., CO-MED report. J Affect Disord 2015;174:157–64 — n=665; self-reported pre-treatment insomnia did not predict response or remission — doi:10.1016/j.jad.2014.11.026. Existing prediction evidence rests on self-report; objective multi-night EEG data in this setting is scarce.
03
Outcome-assessment window and early-change prediction.
Szegedi A et al. J Clin Psychiatry 2009;70:344–53 — meta-analysis, 41 trials, n=6,562; trials run 4–8 weeks; ≥20% improvement within 2 weeks predicts stable response with ≥81% sensitivity — doi:10.4088/jcp.07m03780.
04
VEK processing.
Komitéloven: decision within 60 days of a properly formed application; nationally valid; submitted in the PI's region. Regional committee guidance recommends submitting at least 60 days before the chosen committee meeting. Region Hovedstaden has publicly documented delays beyond the statutory limit.
